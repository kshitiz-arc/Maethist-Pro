MAETHIST has no accounts, no sign-up and no server-side copy of your work. That makes this document short, and it makes most of it a list of things that do not happen. Each paragraph below describes the code as it runs today.

What we collect

Nothing that identifies you, by default. There is no account, no email field and no password anywhere on this site.

On your device, and only there

Your notes, drawings, pages, notebooks, saved video moments and every preference live in your own browser, in localStorage and IndexedDB (the database inkyscib_cache, whose records store holds your notebooks and preferences and whose documents store holds any PDF or deck you imported), a picture you choose as the canvas background, in the database inkyscib_background, and any lesson you record, in the database inkyscib_lessons. None of it is uploaded, and there is no endpoint on the server that could receive it.

The microphone. When you record a lesson "with voice", your browser asks for the microphone. The sound is recorded by your browser and saved with the lesson in inkyscib_lessons, on your device. It is never sent to this server or to anyone else, and nothing listens when you are not recording. "Record ink only" never asks. The site's permissions policy allows the microphone and camera for this site alone. If you clear your browser storage, it is gone, and we cannot bring it back.

What passes through the server, and does not stay

  • A PDF or PowerPoint file you import. PDFs and .pptx files are normally converted in your own browser and never reach the server. When the server does convert one, it turns it into page images and returns them to your browser. A PDF is read in memory and never written to disk; a PowerPoint file is written into a temporary directory that is deleted when the request ends, whether the conversion succeeded or failed (tempfile.TemporaryDirectory in app.py). It is never copied anywhere else, and the same is true of the original PDF when you export annotations back onto it. PowerPoint export never touches the disk at all.
  • An image you convert to text. When you use Image to text, the part of the page you chose (or the picture you picked) is sent to this server, which reads it with two open models running on the server itself (Pix2Text MFR for equations, TrOCR for handwriting) and sends the text back. The image is read in memory and dropped when the request ends. It is never written to disk, logged, kept, or sent to any other company.
  • A lecture search. The words you type are sent to the server, which asks YouTube on your behalf, so your own address is not sent to Google for the search. The result is held in the server's memory for ten minutes, keyed by the words you typed and attached to nobody.
  • A saved video moment. The server reads the frame you asked for from the video's public YouTube stream, at up to 1080p (YouTube's own preview of that moment first, so something appears at once), and passes it to your browser, where it is stored. Only the few seconds of video around that moment are read, and nothing is written to disk. Nothing about which video you watched is recorded.

What the server keeps

  • Web server logs — the request line, the status code and how long it took. Before any log record is written, a filter rewrites IP addresses (IPv4 loses its last octet, IPv6 is cut to a 48-bit prefix), email addresses and document filenames out of it, so the identifying version never reaches a disk or a log service (logging_filters.py).
  • A visit count — how many times each page was loaded each day, split into phone, tablet and desktop, and which website a visitor followed a link from (its name only, such as google.com, never the full address). It is a tally, not a record of visitors: there is no cookie, no identifier, no IP address and nothing that tells one visit from another, so it cannot be linked back to anyone.

No analytics, no tracking, so no cookie banner

There is no analytics service on this site: no Google Analytics, no tracking pixel and nothing that follows you between visits. The visit count above is kept by this server itself and sent nowhere. Because nothing here is stored on your device to measure you, there is nothing for a cookie banner to ask about. Earlier versions offered Google Analytics as an opt-in; version 2 removed it altogether.

Keeping the service up

To stop one visitor flooding the lecture search, video capture or file conversion, the server counts each visitor's requests for one minute. It does so with a scrambled code made from a shortened version of your address (the last part is dropped first), held in memory for that minute only. It is never written to disk or logged, and it changes every time the server restarts.

Why we are allowed to

  • Converting the file you asked us to convert: the request itself. Art. 6(1)(b) where a contract for this free service exists, and Art. 6(1)(f) otherwise. The processing lasts as long as the request.
  • Security and abuse logs, request limits and the visit count: legitimate interests. Art. 6(1)(f). The interest is keeping the site up; the balancing test is what the redaction filter is for, because the logs are stripped of identifiers before they are written.

Where it goes

Two companies can receive anything at all, and one of them only if you ask for a lecture.

Nothing is sold. Nothing is shared for advertising. There is no advertising network, no data broker, no CRM, no email list and no profiling of any kind. Where the YouTube player sends data to Google outside the EEA or the UK, that transfer relies on the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and on Google's standard contractual clauses.

Your rights

For everything that matters — your notes, your drawings, your notebooks and your settings — you do not need to ask us, and you do not have to wait 30 days. The data is on your machine, so access, portability and erasure are two controls on this page. That is also the honest limit of our power: we cannot delete your work for you, because we cannot reach it.

Your right of access and to portability, in a machine-readable file: GDPR and UK GDPR Art. 15 and Art. 20, DPDP §11, CPRA "right to know", LGPD Art. 18(II), PIPEDA Principle 9, Australian Privacy Principle 12 and Singapore's PDPA access obligation.

Your right to erasure: GDPR and UK GDPR Art. 17, DPDP §12(3), CPRA "right to delete", LGPD Art. 18(VI). It clears every store this site uses and cannot be undone, so export a backup first.

Rights we have to handle ourselves

One record is on the server and out of your reach: the redacted web server logs. Ask us about it — access, correction, erasure, restriction, objection, or a copy — and we will answer within 30 days, which is the window GDPR Art. 12(3), the UK GDPR, DPDP §13 and the CPRA all set. Because the logs are stripped of identifiers before they are written, we will usually have to tell you that we cannot find anything belonging to you in them, and that is not an evasion: it is the design.

Which law gives you which right

  • EU and EEA — GDPR. Access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection.
  • United Kingdom — UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018. The same set.
  • India — the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. Access to a summary, correction and completion, erasure, nomination, and grievance redressal (§11–§14). The Act is passed and is being brought into force through rules; this policy follows it as written.
  • California — CCPA as amended by the CPRA. Know, delete, correct, opt out of sale or sharing, and limit the use of sensitive information. We do not sell or share personal information, and we collect no sensitive categories, so there is nothing to opt out of.
  • Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut and Utah — VCDPA, CPA, CTDPA and UCPA. Access, deletion, correction, portability, and opt-out of targeted advertising and profiling. There is no targeted advertising or profiling here to opt out of.
  • Brazil — LGPD. The Art. 18 rights, including confirmation, access, correction, anonymisation, deletion and information about sharing.
  • Canada — PIPEDA. Access to your information and the right to challenge its accuracy and our handling of it.
  • Australia — the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles. Anonymous use is not an option here, it is the only mode: there is no account to create (APP 2), and APP 12 and APP 13 cover access and correction.
  • Singapore — the PDPA. Access, correction, and the retention limits listed above.

Children

MAETHIST is built for students, so this matters more here than on most sites. There are no advertisements, no profiles, no behavioural tracking and no recommendation engine — for anyone, of any age. There is no analytics either.

Under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, everyone under 18 is a child, and tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted advertising directed at children are prohibited outright (§9). Nothing here does any of those things. In the United States, COPPA applies to children under 13; we knowingly collect nothing from anyone, which includes them.

Complaints

Please tell us first, so we can fix it. You do not have to, and you can go straight to a regulator:

  • United Kingdom — the Information Commissioner's Office.
  • EU and EEA — your national supervisory authority, listed by the European Data Protection Board.
  • India — the Data Protection Board of India, once it is constituted under the Act, via the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology.
  • Elsewhere — your own data protection authority; the rights listed above are the ones your law gives you, not ones we grant.

Who we are, and how to reach us

MAETHIST is built and run by one person, Kshitiz Kumar Sinha, in Ranchi, India, who is the data controller — or, in the language of the Indian Act, the data fiduciary. There is no company, no data protection officer and no support desk, because at this size neither GDPR Art. 37 nor the DPDP Act requires one; if that changes, this section changes with it.
