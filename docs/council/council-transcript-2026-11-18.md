QUESTION: MAETHIST is a free, offline-capable browser canvas for maths students (target: ~960px budget Android tablet, finger or stylus; also desktop). The owner says the two most important tools are circle construction (compass) and angle measurement, and wants every tool improved for the best UI/UX. What are the highest-impact concrete improvements a student on a cheap tablet would feel immediately, ranked, and what should NOT be built?

**Response A:**
All hit tolerances are mouse-sized pixel constants, and nothing anywhere checks event.pointerType. Ranked: (1) Touch-sized hit targets, about 30 lines. SNAP_SCREEN_DISTANCE=10 (construction.js:588), PICK_SCREEN_PX=14 (angle.js:18), EXTEND_GRAB 12 / ARC_BODY_GRAB 14 (compass.js), HIT_SCREEN_PX=14 (construction-menu.js), DRAG_SLOP_PX=4 (compass.js:41). Add a hitPx(base, event) helper in geometry-engine.js: about 1.8x for touch, 1.3x for pen. (2) Angle by three points (A, vertex B, C), about 120 lines, falling back to findSnapTarget, then createAngle. (3) A live angle preview in getOverlayState once the first line is picked. (4) A radius readout drawn beside the cursor while dragging, plus a snap ring. (5) Undo granularity: one undo should remove the last arc. Don't build: a draggable protractor or compass skin, gesture-recognised constructions, 5-degree snapping, any new tool before (1).

**Response B:**
(Fresh-eyed 15-year-old.) (1) The angle reading vanishes as soon as it appears: angle.js:160 sets "Marked 47.3°", then line 204 overwrites it with "Click the first line". (2) Hand-drawn lines are ignored. segments() only collects construction segments and shape edges, so a pen V gives "That missed" with no reason. Offer a 3-tap mode that works on ink. (3) "Click" vs "Tap": the JS says Click, the HTML says Tap. Use Tap. (4) The corner is picked for me (directionFrom uses the far endpoint), with no way to flip 130° to 50°. (5) The compass takes three steps; release after setting the radius should draw a full circle by default. (6) The live radius should show next to the finger, not in a panel. Don't build: more arc colours or a hex picker, 4-character point renaming in the compass panel, any hint shown only at 1180px or above.

**Response C:**
The biggest risk is gesture conflicts and mouse-tuned tolerances. (1) DRAG_SLOP_PX=4 is far too small for a finger: taps become drags and commit tiny arcs. Raise it to about 10 for touch via pointerType. (2) lineAt computes pickedAt and then nothing uses it; directionFrom picks the far endpoint, so on an X crossing the mark lands on the wrong corner. (3) In READY, a press checks the arc end, then the arc body, then starts a sweep, so in a perpendicular bisector the second arc reopens or extends the first. Require a long-press for that. (4) Float noise: 59.9° for a constructed 60°. Snap the display when within 0.05°. (5) The radius locks where the finger lifts; snap it or lock on dwell. (6) The angle tool can't measure through points. Don't build: a compass graphic, more snap types, gesture shortcuts, cm calibration. Add touch-jitter tests first.

**Response D:**
The engine is better than the experience. (1) One-gesture compass: press the centre, drag the radius with a live formatLength readout, lift, sweep. Keep the radius, so the next tap on a point draws the same arc (free bisector and equilateral-triangle constructions). (2) Angle by three taps on points, with a live degree value before the second tap. (3) Live linked measures: the angle mark stores sourceIds, so the degrees should update when the points move. (4) A Constructions quick-menu (perpendicular bisector, angle bisector, perpendicular from a point) that plays the real compass steps. (5) An obvious reflex/interior toggle; automatic square mark at 90°. Don't build: a CAS, 3D, a protractor skin, multi-step settings, anything only shown at 1180px or above. Upside: construction replay or a "proof strip" in the PDF export.

**Response E:**
Reframe: the student wants an exact circle and an angle they can read. The code copies physical instruments as multi-step state machines. (1) Measure an angle by tapping three points (∠ABC), not two lines; lineAt needs segments within 14px. (2) Let the student choose which of the four angles: directionFrom picks the far endpoint, and createAngle folds anything over 180° to the minor angle, so 110° can come out as 70°. Tap inside the wedge, tap again to flip. (3) Bigger compass targets (SNAP 10 -> 20-24, slop 4 -> 10). Snap the radius to whole cm or mm with an off switch. Show it at the pen tip, not in syncPanel. (4) In circle mode, release after setting the radius should draw immediately. (5) Stop guessing: in READY a press on any arc reopens or extends it, so require a long-press. Don't build: a protractor or compass image, more construction-menu options, more auto-recognition, animations.
