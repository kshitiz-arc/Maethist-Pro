LLM Council: tool UX for MAETHIST

What are the highest-impact UX improvements to circle construction (the compass) and angle measurement, and to the tool system generally, that a student on a cheap tablet would feel immediately? And what should not be built?

Chairman's verdict

Where the council agrees. The tools are tuned for a mouse on a product built for fingers. Hit radii of 10–14px and a 4px tap-or-drag threshold, with nothing reading pointerType. Angles should be measurable from three tapped points, as in angle ABC. The compass takes too many steps. Readings belong next to the finger, not in a side panel. Do not build an on-canvas protractor or compass skin.
Where it clashes. Order of work: the Expansionist wanted new flows first (a constructions menu, linked live measures), while the others said fix touch first. Touch wins. Radius snapping, lock-on-dwell or keep-the-last-radius: snapping with an off switch first. Reopening an arc: a long-press.
Blind spots caught in review. createAngle folds anything over 180° to the minor angle. directionFrom ignored where you tapped, so the mark could land on the wrong corner. The reading was overwritten in the same tap. Pen ink was rejected without a reason. 59.9° showed for a constructed 60°. Missed by everyone: a maths-correctness test set, palm rejection, typed entry and screen-reader output for readings, and testing on real hardware with students.

The recommendation, and what was built

  1. Hit targets that scale with the input: about 1.8× for touch, 1.3× for a pen. built
  2. A tap-or-drag threshold of 10px for a finger, 6px for a pen, 4px for a mouse. built
  3. The angle reading stays on screen. built
  4. Flip any mark to its reflex angle and back. built
  5. Three-tap angles (arm, corner, arm) that work on ink, PDFs and pictures, with a live reading. built
  6. The mark goes on the corner that was tapped. built
  7. Circle mode draws the circle when the radius drag is released, as one undo step. built
  8. The radius readout sits on the compass opening. built
  9. Hold to pick up an old arc, so a plain press starts a new one. built
  10. Whole degrees when within 0.05°. built
  11. "Tap", not "Click". built
  12. One undo per circle. built
  13. Regression tests for each fix (tests/test_touch_geometry.py, 10 tests). built
  14. Radius snapping to whole cm/mm with an off switch. Not yet built.

Do not build: a protractor or compass image, gesture-recognised constructions, 5° angle snapping, more construction-menu options, more arc colours, a CAS or 3D, cm calibration, decorative animation.

The one thing to do first: touch-aware hit sizes and tap threshold. Every other fix depends on taps registering correctly. This is done.

Advisor responses

The Contrarian

Gesture conflicts and mouse-sized tolerances are the real risk. DRAG_SLOP_PX = 4 turns finger taps into tiny arcs. lineAt computed pickedAt and never used it, so the mark landed on the wrong corner of an X. In READY, a press on an old arc reopened it, which is exactly where the second arc of a bisector starts. 59.9° for a constructed 60°. The radius locked where the finger lifted. There was no three-point angle. Don't build a compass graphic, more snap types, gesture shortcuts or cm calibration. Add touch-jitter tests first.

The First Principles Thinker

The student wants an exact circle and an angle they can read; the code copies physical instruments as multi-step state machines. Measure from three points. Let the student choose which of the four angles they mean, since createAngle folds over 180°. Make the compass targets bigger and snap the radius to whole units. Draw on release in circle mode. Require a long-press before re-editing an arc.

The Expansionist

The engine is better than the experience. A one-gesture compass that keeps its radius gives bisectors and equilateral triangles for free. Three-tap angles with a live value. Live linked measures, because angle marks already store sourceIds. A constructions quick-menu that replays the real steps. A reflex toggle. Upside: construction replay, or a "proof strip" in the PDF export.

The Outsider

The reading vanishes as soon as it appears. Hand-drawn lines are ignored without a reason. The JS says "Click" and the HTML says "Tap". The corner is picked for me. The compass takes three steps where I expect one. Show the radius next to my finger. Don't add arc colours, point renaming in the compass panel, or hints behind a "?".

The Executor

Every hit tolerance is a pixel constant and nothing checks pointerType. One helper and six constant swaps improve every tool at once. Then three-point angles (about 120 lines), a live angle preview, a radius readout while dragging, and one undo per arc. Don't build a skin, gesture recognition or 5° snapping.

Peer review highlights

Show the five reviews
  • General: First Principles was strongest. The Expansionist's blind spot was features before basics. Everyone missed hardware testing, palm rejection and curriculum precision.
  • Teacher: First Principles was strongest because it caught the maths error. The Executor treated teaching as engineering. Everyone missed teacher workflow (projecting, setting tasks, export) and a correctness test set.
  • Accessibility: The Executor was strongest. The Expansionist ignored finger occlusion and motor load. Everyone missed keyboard and typed entry, an ARIA live region, colour-only cues and timing settings.
  • Regression engineer: First Principles was strongest. The Expansionist added regression risk. Everyone missed a verification plan and saved-file compatibility.
  • Student: The Outsider was strongest. The Executor was developer-centric. Everyone missed a first-run worked example and homework and exam use.
